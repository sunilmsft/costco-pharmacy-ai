## What This Meeting Really Is

This meeting is both:

- a collaborative product discussion
- an informal assessment of whether I think clearly, understand what I am proposing, answer questions honestly, and would be someone the team wants to work with

I do not need to prove that every prototype decision is correct. I need to demonstrate that I can:

- identify a meaningful opportunity
- structure an ambiguous problem
- distinguish facts, assumptions, and hypotheses
- connect member value with operations, systems, measurement, and risk
- work constructively with domain experts
- acknowledge what I do not know
- improve the idea when challenged

## What They May Be Evaluating

### Do I understand the problem?

Keep returning to the member outcome rather than visual polish or technology. The useful question is: what becomes easier, clearer, more continuous, or more trustworthy for the member?

### Can I explain my choices?

There may not be one correct answer, but I should be able to explain the reasoning behind my decisions, what tradeoff I made, and what I would test next.

### Do I recognize the limits of my knowledge?

Do not invent Costco-specific facts. Clearly distinguish:

- what I observed publicly
- what I assumed
- what I would need to validate

### Can I handle disagreement?

Treat disagreement as useful information rather than something I need to defeat. A challenge may reveal an operational reality, clinical constraint, system boundary, or member need I missed.

### Am I making the conversation useful?

Be curious, listen carefully, include the full room, and make the conversation better. Good judgment includes knowing when to ask, pause, revise, or defer.

## How I Will Answer Questions

### Clarify → Answer → Boundary → Validation

1. **Clarify** the question.
2. **Answer** with my current view directly.
3. **Boundary** what I am not assuming or cannot confirm.
4. **Validation** how I would test or verify it.

Example:

> “Let me play that back to make sure I understood the question correctly.
>
> My current view is that the conversational experience should guide the member and collect intent, while consequential actions remain within deterministic workflows.
>
> I am not assuming those integrations exist today.
>
> I would first validate whether the member outcome is valuable enough to justify the required integration and governance.”

- “That may change how I would shape the experience.”
- “The prototype is making the assumption visible, not claiming that it is correct.”
- “The starting journey should be selected using Costco’s evidence, not my prototype preference.”

## Prototype Technology — Remember Three Things

**What it is → What it isn’t → What comes next**

### What it is

- Browser-based concept
- HTML, CSS, and JavaScript
- Scripted, state-driven interactions
- Sample data
- Built in VS Code
- GitHub Copilot used as a development accelerator

### What it is not

- No live AI model
- No backend or database
- No Costco system integrations
- No live clinical recommendations
- No real transactions

### What comes next

- Validate the member journey first
- Identify the authoritative data and systems required
- Define consent, permissions, clinical boundaries, and governance
- Select the model, platform, and architecture only after the product need is clear

**If asked, “Is this actually AI?”**

“Not in the current runtime. It simulates the intended experience so we can validate the member journey before choosing models, integrations, and architecture.”

**If asked, “Why didn’t you connect a real model?”**

“I wanted to validate whether the journey was valuable first, rather than creating the appearance of a finished technical solution.”

### Simple analogy

- HTML = structure
- CSS = appearance
- JavaScript = behavior

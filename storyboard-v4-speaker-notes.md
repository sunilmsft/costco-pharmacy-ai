## Slide 2 — Strategic Opportunity

### Purpose

Clarify where the opportunity exists. The member's need and the existing Costco Pharmacy workflow may remain unchanged. The opportunity is to improve the journey between intent and successful action.

### Core message

The destination may already exist. The differentiator is making the path more contextual, continuous, efficient, and easier for the member to complete.

### Suggested talk track

"Costco Pharmacy already has many of the workflows members need. The member's goal and the final workflow may therefore remain exactly the same.

What can change is the journey in between: understanding the need, using relevant permitted context while keeping the member in control, carrying progress forward, and moving into the right workflow or to pharmacy staff without making the member restart.

The value is not simply fewer clicks. It is a more continuous, relevant, and easier experience."

### Transition to Slide 3

"How that journey should be designed and measured depends on the outcome Costco Pharmacy most wants to improve."

### Optional detail

- A prescription transfer is one example; the existing workflow remains the destination.
- Existing workflows remain the systems of action.
- Guided support is one possible enabler, not the strategy.
- Potential value includes continuity, completion, relevance, trust, and better human handoff.
- Defaults should remain visible and editable.
- Context should reduce effort, not remove member control.

## Slide 3 — Priority-Led Experience

### Purpose

Show that the proposed approach is not a fixed solution. The journey, starting scenario, and measures should be tailored to what Costco Pharmacy is trying to achieve.

### Core message

The same underlying approach can produce very different experiences depending on whether the priority is growth, member value, operational efficiency, or a combination.

### Suggested talk track

"One assumption I do not want to make is what Costco Pharmacy is trying to optimize for.

If the priority is growth, the experience may focus on making it easier to acquire and retain prescriptions, for example by simplifying transfers or helping members discover relevant pharmacy services.

If the priority is member value, the focus may be convenience, trust, and continuity: helping members complete common needs more easily and carrying context forward when the journey moves into a workflow or to pharmacy staff.

If the priority is operational efficiency, the experience may focus on successful self-service, clearer status and next steps, and reducing avoidable calls while improving the quality of handoffs.

The point is not one fixed assistant or one generic experience. The priority should shape the journey, the starting scenario, and how success is measured."

### Transition to Slide 4

"Once the priority is clear, the next question is how to create that value while preserving trust, member control, and access to pharmacy staff."

## Slide 4 — Trust and Safety

### Purpose

Establish that trust, Responsible AI, member control, clear boundaries, and intentional human support must be designed into the entire experience from the beginning.

### Core message

Clear boundaries, responsible context, and intentional human support are the practical Responsible AI guardrails for a guided pharmacy experience.

### Suggested talk track

"Whatever priority Costco Pharmacy chooses, trust and Responsible AI have to be designed into the experience from the beginning.

I think of that in terms of three practical guardrails.

First, clear boundaries, so the experience supports members without replacing pharmacist judgment.

Second, responsible use of context—only approved information, under defined rules, while preserving privacy, consent, and member control.

Third, intentional human support. When the experience needs to hand off, it should carry useful context forward so the member does not have to restart.

The terminology and governance should align with Costco's own standards, but the important point is that these principles are built in from the start, not added later."

### Transition to Slide 5

"With those guardrails in place, the next question is how to start narrowly, measure value, and decide whether the experience has earned the right to expand."

### Optional detail

- Align with Costco's existing Responsible AI, privacy, security, compliance, and clinical-governance standards.
- Apply Responsible AI principles across design, evaluation, monitoring, escalation, and change management.
- Treat Responsible AI as an ongoing operating discipline, not only a launch review.
- Clear boundaries:
	- Support navigation, guidance, and approved workflows.
	- Do not diagnose or replace pharmacist judgment.
- Responsible use of context:
	- Use only approved and relevant information.
	- Preserve authentication, privacy, consent, auditability, and member control.
- Intentional human support:
	- Design escalation and handoff deliberately.
	- Carry useful context and progress forward when appropriate.

## Slide 5 — Validated Evolution

### Purpose

Show how the experience can evolve through Crawl, Walk, and Run, how the phases differ, how measurement operates throughout, and how evidence gates determine whether broader capability is justified.

### Core message

Begin with the simplest useful experience. Measure value, trust, Responsible AI, and operational feasibility. Let the evidence determine whether to advance, iterate, change direction, or stop.

### Suggested talk track

"With the priorities and guardrails established, I would use a crawl-walk-run approach rather than commit upfront to the most advanced experience.

Guide gets the member to the task. It understands the request, clarifies the options, and connects the member to the right existing workflow or person, but it does not perform the task.

Assist helps the member complete the task. In an authenticated experience, it can carry approved context forward, reduce repeated entry, and support more of the workflow while the member reviews and confirms meaningful actions.

Anticipate surfaces a relevant need before the member has to search for it, using permitted signals, consent, and appropriate safeguards.

Measurement runs throughout every phase. At each evidence gate, we assess value, trust and Responsible AI, and operational feasibility.

Moving forward is only one outcome. The evidence may tell us to iterate, change direction, or stop."

### Transition to Slide 6

"The prototype makes a few of these hypotheses tangible so we can pressure-test where value may exist and what should be measured first."

#### Phase definitions

##### Guide — Crawl

Question answered:

"Where should I go, and what should I do next?"

What it can do:

- Understand the member's request
- Explain relevant options
- Clarify requirements or next steps
- Connect the member to the correct existing workflow or person
- Support public or lower-risk guidance scenarios

What it does not do:

- Prefill authenticated information
- Perform the underlying pharmacy task
- Submit or confirm meaningful actions for the member

Illustrative examples:

- Help determine whether the member needs the refill, transfer, immunization, or price-comparison experience
- Explain what information is needed before beginning a transfer
- Route the member to pharmacy staff when judgment is required

##### Assist — Walk

Question answered:

"Can you help me complete this?"

What it can do:

- Operate within an authenticated experience
- Use approved context and saved information
- Prefill fields while keeping them visible and editable
- Carry information and progress into an existing workflow
- Support more of the task
- Preserve context during a human handoff

What it does not do:

- Remove member confirmation
- Act independently on meaningful pharmacy actions
- Replace pharmacist or clinical judgment

Illustrative examples:

- Carry the source pharmacy and prescription information into the existing transfer workflow
- Suggest the member's usual Costco location while allowing a change
- Prefill permitted account information for member review
- Carry the member's intent and progress into a staff handoff

##### Anticipate — Run

Question answered:

"What might I need next?"

What it can do:

- Use permitted signals and consent
- Surface timely and relevant non-clinical suggestions
- Connect needs across approved member journeys
- Offer an appropriate next step before the member searches

What it does not do:

- Make autonomous clinical decisions
- Diagnose
- Replace pharmacist judgment
- Create uncontrolled or unwanted outreach

Illustrative examples:

- Suggest reviewing refill timing before an upcoming trip
- Surface relevant travel-health information after a travel booking
- Remind a member about a relevant pharmacy action when permitted and useful
- Offer a connected pharmacy path from another Costco journey

#### Evidence-gate criteria

- Meaningful member or business value
- Trust, safety, and Responsible AI acceptance
- Operational feasibility
- Manageable staff and system burden
- Evidence sufficient to justify broader scope

#### Gate outcomes

- Advance
- Iterate
- Change direction
- Stop

#### Fail fast

Fail fast means:

- Test the riskiest assumptions early
- Start with a limited and controlled scope
- Define stop or change criteria before testing
- Avoid scaling experiences that do not demonstrate value or trust
- Preserve learning when an experiment does not succeed

Fail fast does not mean lowering:

- safety standards
- privacy standards
- clinical standards
- quality standards
- Responsible AI standards

#### What experimentation means here

Experimentation means testing a clearly defined experience hypothesis with a limited audience, controlled prototype, usability study, or phased pilot before increasing scope.

Preserve the core illustrative questions:

- Does natural-language guidance help members reach the right workflow faster than standard navigation?
- Does carrying previously provided information into an existing workflow reduce abandonment?
- Do visible, editable defaults reduce effort without reducing member control?
- Does a context-rich staff handoff reduce repetition and resolution time?
- Does clearer status or next-step guidance reduce avoidable pharmacy calls?

Experimentation should focus on:

- experience design
- navigation and routing
- wording and prompts
- visible and editable defaults
- handoff behavior
- continuity across workflows
- timing and relevance of non-clinical suggestions

Experimentation should not focus on:

- diagnosis
- clinical advice
- pharmacist judgment
- medication safety standards
- legal or regulatory obligations

#### What A/B testing means here

A/B testing compares two controlled experience variants to determine which better supports the selected outcome.

Preserve the existing examples:

A/B test 1 - Entry experience

Version A:
Traditional pharmacy navigation

Version B:
Natural-language guided entry

Possible measures:
- time to the correct workflow
- number of steps
- successful completion
- abandonment
- avoidable calls

A/B test 2 - Prescription-transfer handoff

Version A:
Member enters the transfer workflow and re-enters all information

Version B:
The guided experience carries permitted information into the existing transfer workflow

Possible measures:
- completion
- repeated data entry
- errors
- time to completion
- member satisfaction

A/B test 3 - Human handoff

Version A:
Generic transfer to pharmacy staff

Version B:
Handoff includes the member's intent, information already provided, and progress made

Possible measures:
- member repetition
- resolution time
- handoff quality
- staff effort
- member trust

A/B test 4 - Editable defaults

Version A:
No default pharmacy or fulfillment option

Version B:
A relevant default is suggested but remains visible and editable

Possible measures:
- completion time
- frequency of changes
- errors
- satisfaction
- perception of control

A/B test 5 - Travel-related guidance

Version A:
No contextual travel guidance

Version B:
A timely, non-clinical suggestion to review refill timing or travel-health information

Possible measures:
- engagement
- useful-action completion
- dismissal or opt-out
- trust
- unintended confusion

Use appropriate consent, privacy, Responsible AI, legal, clinical, and experimentation-governance reviews before any production test.

Not every hypothesis must be tested through a live production A/B test.

Depending on traffic, risk, consent, privacy, and operational feasibility, validation may instead use:

- prototype testing

- controlled pilots
- phased rollout
- qualitative member feedback
- operational measurement

#### Important framing

- Crawl, Walk, and Run describe increasing capability, not fixed dates.
- This is an evolution and validation model, not a committed roadmap.
- Run is not automatically the desired end state.
- Costco Pharmacy may remain at any phase if that phase delivers sufficient value.
- Advancement is never automatic.
- Exact hypotheses, experiments, measures, and thresholds should be selected based on Costco Pharmacy's chosen priority and existing baseline.
